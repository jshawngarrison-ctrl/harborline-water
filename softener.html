The honest Lowcountry hardness story

Charleston Water System’s FAQ reports average hardness around 58.4 ppm / 3.4 gpg — that is moderately soft. Mount Pleasant hardness is generally low to moderate as well. Softeners are excellent tools for hard water; they are easy to oversell here.

Pushing a softener on already-soft municipal water is a reputation risk. Harborline leads with catalytic carbon for chloramine and taste, and treats softeners as a secondary option.

When we do recommend a softener

  • On-site hardness testing shows meaningfully hard water
  • Private well or rural supply with elevated hardness
  • Visible scale on fixtures paired with lab/strip evidence
  • You specifically want salt softening after seeing the numbers

Bottom line: If your free test shows soft-to-moderate water, we will recommend filtration — not a softener — and explain why.

Will you refuse to sell me a softener?

We will not hard-sell one. If you still want a softener after seeing moderately soft results, we can discuss options — but we will document the hardness reading so you decide with eyes open.

What about salt-free conditioners?

Some competitors market salt-free / HALO-style systems at higher public price points (often $5k+). We focus on catalytic carbon value for chloramine. Ask us on the free test if you want a comparison for your home.
